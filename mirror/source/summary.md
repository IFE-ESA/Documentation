# Summary

Development of the IFE-TIMES-Norway energy system model began in 2017 through a collaboration between the Institute for Energy Technology (IFE) and the Norwegian Water Resources and Energy Directorate (NVE). This report documents version 5 of the model, dated September 2026, and describes the updates implemented since the previous model documentation was published in 2024. IFE-TIMES-Norway builds on earlier generations of Norwegian energy system models, including TIMES-Norway, developed in 2009, and MARKAL-Norway, developed in 1992. The model is continuously updated to incorporate methodological advances, new data, and developments in the Norwegian energy system.

IFE-TIMES-Norway is a long-term optimisation model of the Norwegian energy system developed using the TIMES modelling framework, short for The Integrated MARKAL-EFOM System. TIMES is a bottom-up modelling framework that provides a detailed techno-economic representation of energy resources, energy carriers, conversion technologies, infrastructure, and energy-service demands. The model determines the combination of investments and operational decisions that minimises the total discounted cost of the energy system while satisfying projected demands for energy services and other model constraints. The total system cost includes investments in energy-supply and end-use technologies, operation and maintenance costs, the cost of electricity imported from countries outside the model, and revenues from electricity exports.

IFE-TIMES-Norway provides a technology-rich representation of the Norwegian energy system. Onshore Norway is divided into five regions corresponding to the current electricity bidding zones. The geographical representation also includes five offshore oil and gas regions and five offshore wind regions. The model determines operational and investment decisions from the base year of 2018 to 2050. Within this horizon, the model includes periods at five-year intervals from 2020 onwards.

To represent variations in energy production and consumption within each year, every model period is divided into 96 sub-annual time slices. These comprise four representative seasons, each described by 24 hourly time slices. The model includes a detailed representation of energy end use, with energy-service demands divided into numerous categories across the industrial, building, and transport sectors.
