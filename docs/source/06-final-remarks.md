# Final remarks

This report describes the basic version of IFE-TIMES-Norway as of September 2026. The model is continuously under development. In different projects and analyses, scenario files with other data are included. Some examples of projects and analyses can be found in:

- {u}`The Integrated Transport and Energy Modelling (ITEM)` project has applied a two-pronged strategy consisting of (i) an in-depth analysis of the two most important transport corridors in Norway: Oslo-Bergen and Oslo-Trondheim, and (ii) a national modelling framework integrating energy system models with improved vehicle, travel, and freight demand models. Relevant publications: [IFE-E-2021-002.pdf (unit.no)](https://ife.brage.unit.no/ife-xmlui/bitstream/handle/11250/2975933/IFE-E-2021-002.pdf?sequence=1) and [{u}`Modelling the interaction between the energy system and road freight in Norway | Elsevier Enhanced Reader`](https://reader.elsevier.com/reader/sd/pii/S1361920922003959?token=565B966A2D537E3C61C473A21660FFCB8A3951F14E4D25E5188DB50006F86A304C839ECE42E1827D35B90B8B5F470E37&originRegion=eu-west-1&originCreation=20230104092647)

- {u}`ASSETS (Assessment of the value of Flexibility Services from the Norwegian Energy System):` Stochastic programming was used to represent variable renewable energy generation in long-term energy system models. The IFE-TIMES-Norway model was solved with three scenario-generation methods, in which the quality of the corresponding model value was evaluated by stability tests. Publication: [{u}`Stochastic modelling of variable renewables in long-term energy models: Dataset, scenario generation & quality of results - ScienceDirect`](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360544221016637)

- {u}`Norwegian Energy Roadmap:` IFE-TIMES-Norway was linked with the EMPS power market model to provide long-term investment strategies that explicitly consider the operational complexity of the power sector. The linkage was designed to improve the modelling of hydropower generation and external electricity markets in IFE-TIMES-Norway. Publication: [{u}`Bidirectional linkage between a long-term energy system and a short-term power market model (unit.no)`](https://ife.brage.unit.no/ife-xmlui/bitstream/handle/11250/2650245/Seljom_et_al_Energy_198_2020.pdf?sequence=2&isAllowed=y)

- Flexbuild: The project focused on the role of end-use flexibility and its impact on the future energy system in Norway. The IFE-TIMES-Norway model was first linked with a European power market model (EMPIRE) to enable investment strategies at the national level that capture the impact and interaction with the European power system. [{u}`Long-term optimization of the Norwegian energy system under the influence of the European power market | IEEE Conference Publication | IEEE Xplore`](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10161972)

  The model was further used to analyse the role and value of end-use flexibility in the Norwegian low-carbon energy transition, including its impact on sectoral profits and costs. A stochastic modelling framework was used to capture how the role of end-use flexibility in buildings depends on the uncertainty of future European electricity prices. [{u}`The effect and value of end-use flexibility in the low-carbon transition of the energy system - ScienceDirect`](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360544224002263?via%3Dihub)

- Norwegian Centre for Energy Transition Strategies (NTRANS): The centre focuses on the role of the energy system in the decarbonization of sectors such as energy, transport, industry, and buildings, as well as our everyday lives.

  - As part of the centre, four energy transition pathways have been developed, in which the IFE-TIMES-Norway model is used (along with the regional economic model REMES) to analyse the impact of the different pathways on the energy system and the economy. The scenarios capture different levels of technological and societal change, and is frequently used in other projects where IFE-TIMES-Norway is applied. [{u}`Transition pathways as “inter-disciplinary meeting place”`](https://www.slideshare.net/IEA-ETSAP/transition-pathways-as-interdisciplinary-meeting-place)

  - Energy service demand projections are developed for energy use in buildings, industry and transport and applied to the previously described NTRANS scenarios NTRANS Energy demand projections IFE-E-2024-006 {cite}`87`

  - The four energy transition pathways have been analysed further in light of the sustainable development goals (SDGs) in the book chapter “Transition Pathways for a Low-Carbon Norway: Bridging Socio-technical and Energy System Analyses” [{u}`https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-58897-6_9`](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-58897-6_9). In the study, sensitivities on the impacts of potential bottlenecks for the energy transition were performed using parametric runs in IFE-TIMES-Norway.

- The role of offshore wind in the low-carbon transition in Norway has also been analysed with IFE-TIMES-Norway in the book chapter “Clean and Affordable Norwegian Offshore Wind to Facilitate the Low-Carbon Transition” [{u}`https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-58897-6_7`](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-58897-6_7). The work included an expansion of the offshore wind regions in Norway, with more detailed assumptions on geographical distribution, including technical and economic parameters for each wind farm and interconnectors.
